## Saved-scene trajectory selection and descendant backtracking

### Reported defect and diagnosis

- Investigated a reopened Step 4B-era MRB with two trajectories where changing
  the Step 4A selector did not visibly identify the newly selected same-tooth
  line, and deleting trajectories left Step 4C/5 descendants behind.
- The exact user-named `step4b.mrb` was not present under the repository data
  tree. Existing scenes and a focused synthetic MRB reproduced the important
  architecture: two role-owned trajectories can share one target, while the
  active workflow parameter node holds one Step 4C/5 derived branch.
- Root causes were: same-tooth trajectories received identical emphasis;
  selection did not explicitly reveal/centre the chosen line; the selector was
  not role-filtered; and `deleteTrajectoryNode()` removed only the line plus
  display/storage auxiliaries without traversing downstream MRML references.

### Implementation

- Filtered the Step 4A selector to
  `DENTOBOT.TrajectoryRole=EntryToTarget` and synchronized it from the persisted
  parameter reference during planning refresh.
- The exact selected trajectory is now visible, labelled, strongly emphasized,
  and used to centre the slice views. Same-target siblings are dimmed. Managed
  FDI/ordinal/state names distinguish, for example, `Trajectory 1 [Complete]`
  from `Trajectory 2 [Empty]` after scene reload.
- Added reference-driven impact discovery for the active Step 4C, Step 5A,
  Step 5B, legacy Step 5B/5C, and unified Step 5C branches. The controller asks
  once before unlock, interactive correction, point undo, clearing, deletion,
  or target switching and lists affected stages.
- Per-trajectory cleanup preserves unrelated trajectories, authoritative
  segmentation, target/bounds, support-tooth choices, and a draft support model
  that does not reference the trajectory. It removes only reached descendants
  and only the deleted trajectory's repeated guide selection. Target switching
  removes the whole active derived branch and all branch-local guide selections.
- Logic-level trajectory deletion enforces the same cascade. Legacy/partially
  migrated derived nodes that fail a modern role validator use a bounded
  fallback restricted to exact impacted nodes already held by the active
  parameter node; no display-name deletion was introduced.
