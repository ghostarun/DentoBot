## 15:38 IST — preserve the selected MPR plane during trajectory correction

### Report and diagnosis

- The developer's first live review found the MPR useful but identified a
  fatal correction flow: after choosing an angle, dragging Entry or Target
  caused the MPR and visible points to reorient abruptly instead of allowing
  correction in that selected view.
- Source inspection confirmed every point event called
  `computeTrajectorySliceMatrix()` with the unchanged slider number but a new
  Entry→Target direction. That rebuilt transverse axes from world `+Z`/`+Y`,
  so the numeric angle did not preserve the previously viewed plane.
- Installed Slicer 5.10 headers were inspected read-only in the existing
  container and confirmed `PointStartInteractionEvent` and
  `PointEndInteractionEvent`. The first lookup used `rg`, which was absent in
  the container; the bounded retry used `grep` and succeeded. Slicer was not
  launched.

### Correction

- The selected slice is held unchanged throughout an active Markups drag so
  the control point stays under the pointer. Point and measurement state still
  update; the reslice is scheduled once the interaction ends.
- Added a reusable transport calculation. It projects the previous plane
  normal onto the corrected trajectory's perpendicular space, reconstructs
  slice X and the longitudinal trajectory Y, and applies only the change from
  the last slider angle. Therefore an edit made in the displayed plane keeps
  its exact normal. If the new trajectory is parallel to the previous normal,
  the previous horizontal axis provides a stable finite fallback.
- Selecting another trajectory or enabling the feature starts from the
  deterministic absolute frame. **Reset Orientation** also deliberately
  returns to deterministic world-reference 0°.
- Updated the Step 4A tooltip and the controlled architecture, decision, plan,
  task, changelog, and logbook records. No environment or dependency changed,
  so `SETUP.md` was not modified.

### Request and confirmed interaction contract

- The developer rejected the initial Step 5C isolation UX. The fixed anterior
  camera, simultaneous 2D/3D layout, and camera lock made editing chaotic and

  time and uses the Step 5B ROI as a traditional XYZ frame.
- The developer confirmed zero yaw as camera look direction ROI `+Y`, ROI `+X`
  to viewport right, ROI `+Z` to viewport up, and yaw about ROI `+Z`. The ROI
  top and bottom must meet the 3D viewport top/bottom boundaries.
- Translation, zoom, pitch, and roll are locked by default so native 3D motion
  becomes a 360-degree yaw-only turntable. A second checkbox freezes yaw too;
  clearing the first lock deliberately enables a free camera.
- The developer also required the Step 4A target bounds and Step 5B shell ROI
  to be completely workflow-generated and non-customizable in slice/3D views.

### Diagnosis and implementation

- Inspection found Step 5B explicitly called `SetLocked(False)`. Step 4A set
  the node locked and hid handles at creation, but did not disable view
  selection or reapply the policy to all loaded nodes. The old camera observer
  also rewrote orientation on every camera Modified event while keeping the
  existing multi-view layout.
- Replaced isolation with a one-up 3D workspace. It captures the prior Slicer
  layout, 3D camera, crosshair mode, and display visibility, then restores all
  of them on explicit exit, module exit, scene close, or widget cleanup.
  Isolation hides unrelated nodes and does not create a plane/curve or start
  Markups placement; the edit buttons remain separate deliberate actions.
- Derived an orthonormal camera frame from the current Step 5B ROI. The camera
  position is an ROI-Z yaw orbit around the ROI center; parallel scale is
  exactly half the ROI Z size. A live frame label reports XYZ convention,
  current yaw, and yaw-only/frozen/free mode. The isolated viewport also shows
  Slicer's XYZ axes marker and restores the prior marker/axis-label state on
  exit.
- Reworked the camera observer to coalesce correction to one event-loop task,
  capture only the yaw component, and set only camera properties that drifted.
  The default lock restores focal point, orbit distance, ROI-Z view-up,
  parallel projection, and scale while preserving yaw. Yaw lock preserves the
  captured angle too.
- Added one reusable ROI interaction policy: node lock on, view selection off,
  and translation/rotation/scale handles off. It applies to new, reset, and
  loaded Step 4A/5B role-owned ROIs. Automatic Step 5B generation resets the
  ROI to axis-aligned Step 5A bounds before creating the shell. The existing
  `TemplateShellTrimROI` role remains for MRB compatibility even though its
  earlier user-adjustable meaning is superseded.
